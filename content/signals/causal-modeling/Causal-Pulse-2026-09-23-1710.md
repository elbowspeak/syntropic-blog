- **What is shifting**:

An $89.4M base is also hard to reconcile with the enterprise customer list above, which suggests the definition of "causal AI" is broad or inconsistent.
- **Causal ML vs causal inference**: Most near-term commercial pull in this data is causal ML (better prediction, interpretability, scenario simulation). The higher-value and harder-to-sell mechanism-level inference appears in pharma, root cause analysis and effect estimation.

## Predictions

These are extrapolations from the signals above, not facts from the findings.

1. **PyWhy becomes the default open-source substrate.** Corporate-backed libraries without a commercial wrapper will be discontinued more often. Expect more migration guides away from CausalNex.
2. **"Causal as agent guardrail" becomes the dominant vendor pitch within about 12 months.** causaLens is already positioned this way, and Causal-Copilot points the same direction.
3. **Automated discovery reduces expert labor but not assumption risk.** Foundation models and agents will lower the cost of proposing graphs. Validating the assumptions behind a graph stays a human-accountable step, which is the likely locus of regulatory scrutiny.
4. **Regulatory guidance is the pharma gate.** Commercial expansion there will track guidance, not model accuracy.
5. **Market forecasts get revised.** Analyst figures with a ~72% CAGR and inconsistent adoption denominators are unlikely to survive contact with actual deal flow.
