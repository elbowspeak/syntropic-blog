## Market Position

Causal AI is bifurcating into two tracks moving at different speeds. **Causal ML** (using causal structure to improve prediction/recommendation) is graduating into production at hyperscale — Meta's ads work is the clearest evidence of this. This track doesn't need a "causal AI" brand; it's being absorbed into existing ML infra as a technique, which is bad for pure-play causal AI vendors competing on category identity and good for infra/tooling providers who can slot causal methods into existing pipelines.

**Causal inference proper** (understanding mechanisms, estimating intervention effects, supporting regulatory/scientific claims) remains concentrated in high-stakes verticals with regulatory or scientific rigor requirements — drug discovery (Aitia/Ono) and clinical trial design (digital twins, ICH E9 R1 estimands) being the clearest examples this week. This track is slower, more expensive to sell into, but more defensible because domain + regulatory expertise is the moat, not the underlying math (which is increasingly open-sourced via CausalPy, EconML, Tigramite, gCastle).

The standalone "causal AI platform" category (causaLens-style) appears to be losing definitional ground, folding causal reasoning into broader agentic-AI positioning rather than competing as a distinct platform category.

## Predictions

- **Causal methods will keep disappearing into existing ML/analytics stacks** rather than persisting as a distinct product category — expect more "causal-ML-as-a-feature" announcements (like Meta's) and fewer "causal AI platform" launches.
- **Vertical depth (pharma, clinical biostatistics) will outcompete horizontal causal AI platforms** for enterprise dollars, because regulatory estimand frameworks (ICH E9 R1) create durable, hard-to-commoditize demand that generic causal inference tooling can't satisfy alone.
- **Open-source consolidation will accelerate pricing pressure** on any vendor selling "the algorithm" rather than proprietary data, compute, or domain integration — CausalPy/EconML/Tigramite covering most standard methods means differentiation has to come from elsewhere (e.g., Aitia's REFS + proprietary biological data).
