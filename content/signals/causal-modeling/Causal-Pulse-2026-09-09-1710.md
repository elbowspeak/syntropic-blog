## Market Position

Causal AI is bifurcating along a predictable line: **narrow, vertical-specific causal *ML*** (MMM/marketing, DevOps RCA, robotics sample-efficiency) is commercializing now because it has clear ROI, tolerant risk profiles, and no regulatory gatekeeping. **General-purpose causal *inference* platforms** (decisionOS-style tools, clinical-trial causal frameworks) are earlier-stage — technically credible but bottlenecked by either regulatory ambiguity (clinical) or the need for hyperscaler distribution to reach enterprise budgets (causaLens/Google Cloud).

The tooling layer is consolidating rather than expanding: a name-brand consulting-backed library (CausalNex) died while the more community/vendor-neutral PyWhy stack persists — a sign the open-source causal inference stack is maturing into a smaller set of standard libraries, which usually precedes (not follows) serious platform-layer investment.

The one hard adoption number in the findings — MMM at 27%, incrementality testing at 28% — indicates causal measurement in marketing is real but still a minority practice, and enterprises are choosing the cheaper, less rigorous method (MMM) over the more causally sound one (incrementality testing) at similar adoption rates, suggesting cost and complexity, not conviction, are still gating factors.
