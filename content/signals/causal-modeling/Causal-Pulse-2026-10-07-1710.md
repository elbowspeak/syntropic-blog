## Market Position

Causal AI is exiting the "research curiosity" phase and entering early commercial scaling — but unevenly across use cases. Marketing measurement (MMM) is the furthest along: it has a quantified market size, a named adoption percentage (69%), and a specific pain point CMOs will pay to solve (financial impact attribution). This is the first vertical where causal inference is being procured as a line-item solution rather than a research methodology.

Pharma/drug development is second-furthest along, but moving on a regulatory timeline rather than a product timeline — adoption is being pulled by FDA/ICH guidance rather than pushed by vendor sales motion, which typically means slower but more durable demand.

Insurance/financial services (CausaLens/Aviva) and supply chain (DACRI) are earlier-stage: real paying customers exist, but the findings show single-vendor, single-customer proof points rather than market-wide adoption data.

The open-source tooling layer (gCastle, Tigramite, Microsoft's DoWhy/EconML/Causica/ShowWhy suite) is maturing toward end-to-end pipelines (discovery → estimation → mediation), which typically precedes a wave of vertical-specific commercial products built on top of de-risked infrastructure — this is a leading indicator, not yet a lagging one.

What's shifting: the field is bifurcating into (1) causal ML — using causal structure to make predictions more robust/transferable (MMM, drug repurposing) and (2) causal inference proper — using causal models to understand mechanisms for accountability and governance (the AI Ethics/public-sector paper, pharma regulatory estimands). Investors and buyers conflate these, but they have different sales cycles, different buyers, and different defensibility.
