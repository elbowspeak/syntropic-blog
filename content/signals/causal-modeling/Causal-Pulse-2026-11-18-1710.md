## Market Position

Causal AI is past the "is this real" phase and into "who captures the value" phase, but unevenly across its two branches:

- **Causal ML (prediction improvement via causal structure)** — e.g., MMM, anomaly scoring, root-cause analysis — is commoditizing fast. Google and Meta give it away (Meridian, Robyn); Adobe is absorbing it into existing suites rather than selling it standalone; DoWhy's incremental improvements (missing data, anomaly scoring) are closing production gaps that previously required custom engineering. This branch is becoming table-stakes infrastructure, not a venture-scale product category on its own.
- **Causal inference / intervention design (understanding mechanisms, prescribing actions)** — the harder, more valuable branch — remains largely unsolved at the LLM-automation layer (InterveneBench), which is where the real commercial upside lives: "what intervention should we make" is worth more than "what will happen if nothing changes." Bareinboim lab's 2026 output (algorithmic recourse, imitation learning, world-model transfer) is the research frontier for this branch, with no clear productization yet.

The practical result: capital is flowing to vertical applications of causal methods (robotics world models, semiconductor fab diagnostics, AI SRE) rather than horizontal "causal AI platform" plays — a sign the market has concluded horizontal causal AI platforms (the causaLens model) face commoditization pressure from Google/Meta/Microsoft giving away the core math.

## Predictions

- Expect more incumbents (SAP, Salesforce, Snowflake) to follow Adobe's pattern — folding causal/MMM capability into existing analytics products rather than enterprises buying a standalone "causal AI platform." This compresses the TAM for pure-play causal AI vendors at the marketing/analytics layer specifically.
- Vertical causal AI (robotics, semiconductors, AI SRE/ops) will keep outraising horizontal platforms, because the willingness-to-pay for root-cause accuracy in a fab or a production incident is far higher than for marketing attribution, where free alternatives exist.
- The "causal AI" label itself may start to fade from marketing material (see Traversal) as companies find category-specific language ("AI SRE," "self-driving production systems") converts better with enterprise buyers than "causal AI" — this creates a labeling/attribution problem for anyone trying to size the causal AI market by name-based searches alone.
- Intervention design automation (the InterveneBench gap) is the most likely locus of the next genuine causal AI breakthrough, not prediction accuracy — watch for multi-agent systems (STRIDES-style) attempting to productize this in 2027.
