## Market Position

Causal AI is bifurcating by maturity. **Marketing measurement** (MMM, incrementality, attribution) is the one vertical where causal methods have crossed from research into recurring enterprise spend — Adobe's product fold-in and the BCG/Lifesight triangulation data both point to causal measurement becoming table-stakes budget infrastructure, not a differentiator. This is largely **causal ML** (improving allocation decisions) rather than deep **causal inference** (explaining why a mechanism works), which is an easier sell and explains why it commercialized first.

**General-purpose causal decision platforms** (Alembic) are raising at large valuations on the promise of broader enterprise decision simulation, but there's a valuation-to-evidence gap: no customer counts, revenue, or case studies accompany the $645M figure. This looks more like a bet on causal AI as the "next layer" after generative AI hype cools, rather than proven product-market fit outside marketing.

**Open-source infrastructure is consolidating**, with McKinsey's exit (CausalNex EOL) leaving Microsoft-backed DoWhy/EconML as the dominant maintained stack — a quiet but structurally important shift, since the library layer determines who can build compatible commercial tooling cheaply.

**Academic work is pushing toward agentic causal reasoning** (InterveneBench, causal evaluation of AI agents) — using LLMs to automate causal study design itself. This is pre-commercial (sub-60% benchmark scores) but is the most interesting long-term bridge: if it matures, it could compress the expert-labor bottleneck that currently gates causal inference adoption outside well-resourced marketing teams.
