## Market Position

Causal AI sits at an **early-majority-of-enablers, pre-majority-of-end-users** stage. The evidence this week shows three concurrent tracks rather than one unified adoption curve:

1. **Tooling/platform layer (causaLens, Aether AI, kausable, PyWhy ecosystem)** — actively fundraising and building open-core go-to-markets. This layer is in classic early-stage platform-war positioning: multiple well-funded entrants, no clear category leader, open source as the land strategy.
2. **Hyperscaler internal build (Amazon)** — the most mature buyers are choosing to build rather than buy, which is typical of a market where the hardest problems (interference, spillover, non-stationarity) aren't yet solved by any vendor product. This is a warning sign for platform vendors targeting large-enterprise ops/supply-chain use cases: the biggest, most sophisticated customers may never become customers.
3. **Vertical point solutions (Causal Data AI, Causaly)** — picking narrow, high-value problems (trial failure, drug discovery) where causal framing has unambiguous ROI, rather than competing horizontally.

What's shifting: causal **ML** (using causal structure to improve robustness/generalization of predictive models — e.g., Aether AI's world models) is pulling ahead of causal **inference** (estimating treatment effects for human decision-making — e.g., classic econometric-style causaLens/Causal Data AI use cases) in investor attention. The world-models framing lets causal methods ride the broader "physical AI"/robotics narrative rather than competing for budget against traditional A/B testing and econometrics teams.
