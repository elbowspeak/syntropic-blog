## Landscape Assessment

The dominant story this week is not a capability breakthrough — it's the widening gap between agent *deployment* and agent *governance*. Production adoption crossed 57% of enterprises, but the 88% pilot-failure rate and 66.7% operational-incident rate show that most organizations are deploying agents faster than they can govern them. Capital is starting to follow that gap: while mega-rounds still flow to application-layer plays (Cognition, Instinct) and orchestration infra (Temporal), a second wave of smaller but pointed raises (AIUC, AIR Security) is explicitly targeting evaluation, auditing, and discovery — the exact functions the 12%-who-succeed cohort report having (named owners, automated evals, narrow scope). On the framework side, consolidation is real (AutoGen → maintenance mode) rather than proliferation, and MCP's move to a stateless architecture is a genuine infrastructure maturation step, even as the ecosystem still lacks standardized audit trails — a gap enterprises will not tolerate much longer given the incident rate above. Multi-agent swarm research remains active (SwarmBench, HiveMind, ANTS 2026) but stayed almost entirely in the research domain this week — nothing in the findings indicates swarm architectures are shipping to production, and the benchmark data (SWE-EVO's 25% vs. 72.8% collapse on long-horizon tasks) suggests even single-agent systems aren't ready for the sustained, multi-file reasoning that swarm advocates promise to eventually solve.
