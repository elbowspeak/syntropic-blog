## Landscape Assessment

The ecosystem is bifurcating into two clear tracks: **capability/orchestration** (where LangGraph is consolidating as the production default as AutoGen exits, and funding is flowing into infrastructure at scale — LangChain's $3B round) and **governance/security** (where deployment data shows the field is badly out over its skis — 88% incident rate against 14.4% full approval — and acquisition capital from CrowdStrike and Palo Alto is flowing in to close that gap). The swarm/multi-agent research this week (Google's 260-config study, the Minecraft collapse) is a useful corrective to 2025-era "more agents" enthusiasm: coordination is now empirically a double-edged tool, helpful on parallelizable tasks and actively harmful on sequential ones. Benchmark fragmentation (SWE-bench Pro vs. unnamed later variants) suggests the industry's shared yardsticks are losing comparability faster than new ones are being standardized, which should make anyone citing a single leaderboard number nervous.

## Predictions

- Expect agent-security tooling (MCP gateways, runtime guardrails, tool-call scanners) to become a standard line item in enterprise agent RFPs within 2–3 quarters, given the incident-rate data — this will likely show up as a requirement before it shows up as a budget line.
- Multi-agent system design will increasingly branch by task topology: parallelizable analytical work (finance, research synthesis) will keep adding agents; sequential/planning-heavy workflows (software delivery pipelines, operations) will trend back toward fewer, more capable single agents with tool access, not swarms.
- DeepSeek's "Super-agent" model (ambient, cross-session monitoring rather than session-bound) is a plausible template other labs converge on; watch for Anthropic/OpenAI session-persistence or cross-session messaging features to expand in response, following the pattern already seen in Claude Code's August 2026 ListAgents/SendMessage additions.
