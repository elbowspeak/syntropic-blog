## Infrastructure Assessment

Grid-scale battery deployment in the US is genuinely accelerating — Q1 2026 was a record quarter, and front-of-meter (utility/merchant) projects are now >75% of volume, meaning storage is increasingly being deployed as grid infrastructure rather than behind-the-meter backup. Texas's 53% share of 2026 forecast capacity confirms storage is following where interconnection and merchant price volatility are easiest, not necessarily where policy incentives are strongest. The real bottleneck this week isn't cell supply — it's HVDC converter lead times (24–36 months) and the slower-moving transmission buildouts in India, Brazil, and the US that are needed to actually use the power storage and renewables are generating. LDES remains the weak link in the duration stack: deployment is growing but tracking nowhere near the scale needed for a fully renewable grid, and lithium-ion's cost advantage is squeezing out the long-duration chemistries that are supposed to fill that gap.

## Predictions

- **Lithium price volatility returns in 2026–2027**: the shift from oversupply to a forecast 80,000-tonne deficit suggests spot prices will likely move off the ~$18,000/tonne floor within the next 6–12 months, pressuring cell-maker margins and potentially slowing the LFP cost curve that utility-scale storage economics depend on.
- **Sodium-ion stays a niche, not a mainstream LFP replacement, through 2026**: at ~2x LFP cost, expect sodium-ion volume to concentrate in cold-climate and safety-critical applications rather than broad grid-storage adoption this year.
- **HVDC becomes the visible constraint on renewable integration before storage does**: watch for project delays tied to converter lead times rather than battery supply in 2026–2027 utility procurement cycles.
- **LDES Council's 2031 COD deadline concerns (PJM) foreshadow broader timeline slippage**: expect long-duration storage project timelines to be pushed in multiple ISO markets as the gap between policy deadlines and actual LDES development cycles becomes harder to ignore.

## Investment Implications

- **CATL's structural lead is widening, not narrowing** — at 39.9% share and a 2030 pipeline exceeding BYD + LGES combined, CATL is becoming the single point of concentration risk for any Western supply chain diversification thesis. Investors in non-Chinese cell manufacturing (especially Korean majors, now outside the global top 10) should treat current valuations with caution unless there's a clear differentiated moat (specialty chemistry, defense-qualified supply, or domestic content requirements under 45X).
- **US-based critical minerals plays (e.g., Lithium Americas) now carry a government-backing premium** following the ~10% federal equity stake — this changes the risk profile from pure commodity exposure to quasi-sovereign-backed exposure, which the market may not be fully pricing given how recent the precedent is.
- **HVDC equipment and converter suppliers look underweighted relative to the attention battery names get.** With 24–36 month lead times and concentrated supply, this is a classic infrastructure bottleneck play — demand visibility (India, Brazil, MISO/SPP/PJM HVDC studies) is strong and multi-year, but the raw findings don't confirm specific public tickers or margins, so this is a thesis to validate with company-level data, not a named recommendation.
- **LDES technology risk is real and underappreciated**: non-lithium LDES chemistries (iron-air, flow, thermal) are competing against an incumbent (Li-ion) whose cost curve keeps improving. - **Texas/ERCOT merchant storage exposure deserves a closer look** given it's absorbing 53% of 2026's forecast 24 GW — but merchant storage economics are more volatile than contracted utility storage, so this is a higher-beta way to play the same growth trend as front-of-meter utility names.
