## Infrastructure Assessment

Deployment momentum remains strong and is broadening in duration profile: US utility-scale storage hit 52 GW with a record GWh quarter, and 2026's planned additions (~24 GW) nearly double 2025's. But the system is bifurcating — lithium-ion short-duration storage keeps scaling on cost declines (>90% since 2010 per IEA), while LDES technologies (iron-air, flow, thermal, CAES) remain only 6% of total storage and depend on either hyperscaler-backed megadeals (Form Energy/Google) or state mandates (Virginia) rather than market pull. The real bottleneck this week isn't generation or even storage manufacturing capacity — it's transmission. Interconnection queues of 5+ years and 7-10 year transmission build timelines, against 2-year data center build cycles, are the binding constraint, and that gap is pushing some data center operators toward on-site gas generation rather than grid connection. Policy remains a two-edged factor: OBBBA preserved storage tax credits through 2029 with a phase-down, but rising foreign-entity compliance costs are a growing headwind, while state-level mandates (Virginia, Illinois) are starting to backfill federal uncertainty.

## Predictions

- Expect more hyperscaler-anchored LDES deals in the Form Energy/Google mold over the next 12 months — data center operators have capital and urgency that utility IRPs lack, and this becomes the primary demand channel keeping iron-air/flow battery developers alive against lithium-ion cost pressure.
- The utility-owned-vs-third-party-aggregator fight playing out in Minnesota's MISO docket will likely be replicated in other ISOs in 2027; watch for FERC Order 2222 implementation disputes as the proxy battleground.
- Given the transmission build-time mismatch (7-10 years vs. 2-year data center cycles), expect continued growth in behind-the-meter storage and on-site/gas hybrid solutions as a stopgap, with grid-enhancing technologies (reconductoring, dynamic line rating) getting disproportionate policy attention relative to their capital intensity (DOE SPARK's $1.9B as an early marker).
- Cobalt's DRC export cap and the pending China export-control decision (expiring Nov 2026) are near-term catalysts for NMC cell cost volatility; LFP-heavy grid storage is comparatively insulated.
