## Infrastructure Assessment

Deployment is accelerating in GWh terms. The U.S. is adding storage at scale, concentrated in Texas, California, and Arizona. Storage is shifting from peak-shaving toward reliability and data-center backup, and distributed fleets are proving they can dispatch at utility scale during stress events.

The bottlenecks are structural rather than chemical. The grid queue backlog is reported at 2,500+ GW, and the IEA is cited as calling for ~50% growth in annual grid investment by 2030 from a ~$400B baseline. The findings also cite a figure of up to $2T in grid modernization by 2030 (NCSL, via a 2025 Yale page), which is older than the window. Interconnection and transmission, not cell supply, are the binding constraints on what gets built.

The momentum shift is that LDES is moving from pilots to contracted backlog, and that data centers have replaced utilities as the most visible buyer. The gap between announced GWh and commissioned GWh remains the main unknown; none of the findings report commissioning data for the Form Energy or hyperscaler contracts.

## Investment Implications

This is analysis, not a recommendation.

- **Multi-day storage is becoming a real contracted market.** Public exposure isn't identified in the findings, since Form Energy is private. Look at public utilities and integrators that bid into these contracts, and check their backlog conversion rates against the announced GWh.
- **Distributed storage aggregation has been validated at dispatch scale.** The 580 MW event suggests VPP revenue models are working. Tesla and Sunrun are named, but the findings don't include their financials, so the valuation case is unverified.
- **The solid-state valuation gap is the clearest disconnect.** WeLion, QingTao, and ProLogium are reported at ¥20B to ¥27.9B and $3.8B, while the pilot timelines run to 2027. Pricing assumes commercialization that hasn't started. The risk is that IPO windows close before production arrives.
- **Cobalt and nickel exposure is becoming more attractive on supply discipline,** conditional on the DRC quota being confirmed. The risk is Indonesian ramp-up, and the "paper surplus" point suggests the market may be overstating physical oversupply.
- **Lithium is turning toward deficit, but the range is too wide to size a position.** Sodium-ion is the main substitution risk to any lithium-deficit thesis.
- **Recycling is a regulatory-driven theme, and lithium recovery is the unpriced gap.** Companies recovering only cobalt, nickel, and copper are positioned for the 2027 targets. Lithium-recovery technology is underdeveloped, which makes it the highest-risk, highest-option-value segment.
- **Risks the market may not be pricing:** a possible funding gap if the Title XVII deadline is real (unverified), and transmission queues that cap how fast announced storage can connect.
