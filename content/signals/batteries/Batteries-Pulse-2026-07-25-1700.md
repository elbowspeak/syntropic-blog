## Infrastructure Assessment

Deployment momentum is real and accelerating on the grid-storage side: US utility-scale additions are on pace to grow ~60% YoY (15 GW → 24 GW), and the UK's 7.6 GW LDES approval is the kind of regulatory unlock that turns backlog into build. The gap between announcement and construction is narrowing for battery manufacturing too — Ford-CATL Michigan and BYD-Greenvolt Poland both moved from MoU/announcement into physical construction phases this year, not just press releases.

The persistent bottleneck remains interconnection, not manufacturing or storage technology: the 2,200 GW queue backlog is unchanged despite Order 2023 reforms, meaning built capacity increasingly sits waiting for grid connection rather than being capacity-constrained. FERC's move to write computational load explicitly into reliability rules this month suggests regulators are starting to treat data-center demand and storage flexibility as linked policy problems rather than separate tracks — worth watching whether this produces faster interconnection pathways for storage co-located with data centers, or just more compliance burden.

On chemistry, the sodium-ion/solid-state narrative is shifting from lab breakthrough to manufacturing-standard territory (China's July 2026 solid-state standard, CATL's sodium-ion EV target), but actual vehicle/grid deployment volumes at scale are still ahead, not behind, the announcements.
