## Infrastructure Assessment

Grid-scale storage is deploying at genuine scale now, not just being announced: LFP pricing under $75/kWh, deployment doubling in 2026, and Q1 2026 posting the strongest first quarter (9.7 GWh) in industry history all point to storage moving from pilot to core grid infrastructure. The bottleneck has shifted from cost to two other constraints: (1) domestic-content compliance under the tightened IRA rules, which will force supply-chain restructuring for any project breaking ground in 2026+, and (2) critical-mineral availability, where DRC cobalt quotas and Chinese lithium market intervention show raw-material access is now as much a geopolitical variable as a market one. LDES remains the clearest gap between ambition and reality — 15 GWh installed against an 8 TW 2040 target is a rounding error, and China's HiTHIUM industrial park suggests the manufacturing lead for next-gen LDES chemistries may consolidate overseas before US/EU LDES players (funded by raises like Antora's $550M) reach comparable scale. Transmission remains the unsexy laggard: aging grids (40% of European transmission lines over 40 years old) and slow HVDC buildout continue to trail storage and generation deployment rates, meaning storage's grid-firming value may be capped by interconnection and transmission bottlenecks regardless of how cheap batteries get.
