## Infrastructure Assessment

Deployment momentum remains strong on paper — 65 GW of US utility-scale storage by year-end, record 2026 additions, and $8.9B in H1 capital — but this week's signals reveal two structural stress points. First, geographic concentration (80% of new capacity in just three states) means the sector's growth story is really three regional stories, each exposed to state-specific interconnection queues and permitting regimes. Second, the Trump administration's foreign-hardware ban lands squarely on top of an industry still dependent on imported batteries, transformers, and inverters — creating a near-term collision between policy-driven onshoring goals and the physical reality that domestic manufacturing capacity for these components isn't yet built out. The bottleneck is shifting from "will capital show up" (it clearly is) to "can hardware clear customs and interconnection queues fast enough to deploy it."

## Predictions

- Expect a visible gap to open between 2026 storage *announcements* and *commissioned* capacity in Q4 2026/Q1 2027 as the foreign-hardware restrictions work through supply chains — watch for project delay disclosures from developers reliant on Chinese-made batteries and inverters.
- Domestic lithium recycling (R3 Lithium, Ascend Elements, Nth Cycle) will account for a small but rapidly growing share of US cathode-grade lithium supply through 2027, easing — but not solving — the cathode-vs-mining capacity imbalance IEA identifies.
- Long-duration storage (iron-air, vanadium flow) will see continued high-profile offtake deals (following Google/Form Energy) as hyperscalers seek multi-day storage for AI data center load, even at efficiency costs lithium-ion doesn't carry.
