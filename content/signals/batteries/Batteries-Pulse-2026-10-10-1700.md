## Infrastructure Assessment

U.S. grid-scale storage is the clearest "actually happening" story this week: 52 GW operational, 10.3 GW added in H1 2026, and a pipeline implying the sector will roughly double by 2028. The bottleneck has shifted from technology to interconnection queues and grid congestion — which is precisely why hyperscalers (Meta, Google) are now contracting dedicated generation-plus-storage directly rather than waiting on utility queues. That's a meaningful structural change: data center demand is now a storage deployment driver in its own right, not just a load forecast assumption.

Long-duration storage (iron-air, LDES broadly) is transitioning from demonstration to early commercial contracts, but the 400x scaling requirement by 2040 versus a 49% YoY growth rate in 2025 shows the gap between "inflection point" framing and the actual pace needed. On the chemistry side, lithium-ion's challengers (solid-state, sodium-ion, metal-hydrogen) are all in the "pilot line / first commercial order" stage — real progress, but none have displaced lithium-ion at scale yet. Grid modernization (HVDC, digital substations) is advancing in pockets (Minnesota Power's $700M 465-mile upgrade, Germany's E.ON digital substations) but remains project-by-project rather than systemic.
