## Market Trajectory

Capital is still flowing into active inference, but it's narrowing toward a specific thesis: **edge/embodied/defense applications (Stanhope AI, DARPA-Aptima)**, not general-purpose software or enterprise SaaS. Meanwhile the one company that had attempted the broader commercial/enterprise bet — Verses AI — appears to be unwinding, despite a licensing deal announced just two months before its operations wind-down. That combination (narrow-niche funding success + broad-bet failure) suggests the market is self-correcting toward where active inference actually has a structural edge (sparse-data, embodied, real-time control problems) and away from where it was being pitched as a general AGI/agentic framework competing with transformer-based approaches.

On the infrastructure side, the pymdp JAX rewrite is a genuine maturation step — it's the kind of unglamorous tooling work that normally precedes, by 12-24 months, any wave of production deployments. But de Vries's own admission that tooling isn't production-ready is a credible brake on near-term enterprise claims. Net: this week's picture is "selective capital discipline + slow-but-real infrastructure progress + one high-profile failure," not a broad commercialization breakout.
